<objective>
Prepare everything the live cutover needs, so the moment a human says yes there
is nothing left to invent: the rollback artefact, the exact ordered DDL, and a
cursor seed that is correct in the one dimension nobody would think to check.

Purpose: D-06 requires the `registrations` cursor to be seeded to the current
timestamp at cutover so none of the nine historical rows is dispatched, and
D-02's "do not touch `dbPoller.ts`" means the seed cannot live inside the poller.
`DBPoller` is constructed unconditionally at `index.ts:52` with no
enable flag, and its cursor defaults to `1970-01-01T00:00:00.000Z`
(`dbPoller.ts:98`). The instant `photobooth.registrations` starts returning rows,
an unseeded poller queues all nine historical registrations at once — sessions
for people who registered in July and are not in the room. Seeding therefore
happens before the process starts, not inside it.

Output: two read-only/local-only operational scripts, the captured rollback, and
the cutover script itself — all committed before any DDL is proposed.
</objective>

<the_cursor_format_finding>
## The seed value's format is load-bearing, and the obvious choice is wrong

`dbPoller.ts:101` filters `registered_at > ?` and the VIEW's `registered_at` is a
**string** (`DATE_FORMAT(a.created_at, '%Y-%m-%d %H:%i:%s.%f')`, plan 06-01), so
MySQL performs a string comparison, not a temporal one.

The obvious seed is `new Date().toISOString()`, matching the existing default's
shape. It is wrong. Those two formats differ at character index 10: the VIEW
emits a space (0x20) where ISO-8601 emits `T` (0x54), and a space sorts first. So

```
'2026-08-11 14:00:01.000000' > '2026-08-11T14:00:00.000Z'   ->   FALSE
```

A registration made one second *after* an ISO-shaped seed compares as *not
greater* and is skipped — and so is every other registration for the rest of that
calendar day, until the date component finally advances past the seed. That is
the entire cutover failing silently on its first guest, in exactly the way
Pitfall 1 fails: no error, no alert, just nobody ever reaching the booth.

Two rules follow, and Task 1 encodes both:
1. The seed is formatted by the same `DATE_FORMAT` expression the VIEW uses.
2. The seed is read from the **MySQL server** (`SELECT DATE_FORMAT(NOW(6), '%Y-%m-%d %H:%i:%s.%f')`),
   not from the Node process's clock, so host clock skew cannot place the cursor
   ahead of or behind the timestamps the VIEW will emit.

The existing `1970-01-01T00:00:00.000Z` default remains correct as a default
purely by luck of its leading `1`, which is less than every plausible `2`.
</the_cursor_format_finding>
