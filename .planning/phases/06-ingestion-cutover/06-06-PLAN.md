Cover, each as its own subsection:

- **What it is and what it depends on.** `photobooth.registrations` is a VIEW,
  not a table, over six `ets_web` tables named individually. `ets_web` and
  `photobooth` share one MySQL instance, which is what makes a cross-database
  view possible at all. There is no application code on this path: the fallback
  chain, the validity filter and the dedup are all SQL.
- **The standing cost, stated plainly.** An upstream `ets_web` migration that
  renames a table or column breaks ingestion on this side with no signal to
  whoever ran it. Nobody upstream knows this view exists. §10 is the only place
  that fact is written down.
- **The files.** `docs/migrations/06-registrations-view.sql` (the DDL),
  `docs/migrations/06-cutover.sql` (the ordered cutover with its post-checks and
  the write-refusal check), `docs/migrations/06-rollback-registrations-table.sql`
  (the captured pre-phase table). Name them by path; do not duplicate their SQL.
- **The grant.** The account resolved by `SELECT CURRENT_USER()` in plan 06-04,
  the single `GRANT SELECT ON photobooth.*`, and why `SQL SECURITY DEFINER` means
  the account needs no `ets_web` grant. Include the `SHOW GRANTS` command an
  operator runs to re-check it, and the deliberate `INSERT` that must be refused.
- **Cursor seeding.** Why it must happen before the process starts (`DBPoller` is
  constructed unconditionally at `index.ts:52`; there is no enable flag), the
  command, and — the part nobody would guess — that the seed must be in the
  VIEW's `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS.ffffff` format read from the MySQL server, because
  the comparison is lexical and an ISO-8601 seed orders wrongly against it.
- **The health probe.** Its interval key, the two failure classes and which one
  alerts, the exact alert payload shape, and the fact that a healthy cycle logs
  nothing. State explicitly that the probe detects schema breakage and not
  business-logic regressions upstream — a change to how `ets_web` cancels tickets
  would pass the probe and still be wrong.
- **The tie detector.** What `total != distinct_registered_at` means, why it is a
  guest-loss risk at a poll-batch boundary, and what to do about it.
- **The name fallback chain.** The five rungs in order, the exact warn line, and
  that the warn arrives within one probe interval rather than at ingest, because
  `dbPoller.ts` is closed to edits. Include the operational note that a guest
  showing as `Khách • <last 4>` means the landing page did not capture a name.
- **A VALID ticket with zero attendance rows.** RESEARCH's Pitfall 3: an upstream
  path can commit `ticket.status = VALID` and then fail to create the attendance
  row, so the guest never appears in the view at all. If a guest reports
  registering and never being called, check for that before assuming an ingestion
  bug. The view cannot see a row that does not exist and cannot compensate.
- **Rate limiting `/status/:jobId`.** The three keys and their defaults, the
  429 body, and why there is no `Retry-After` and no reason field.
- **Accepted risks**, in §9's style: the 40-bit id and what a successful guess
  yields (a name and a queue position, not a photo); distributed enumeration from
  many addresses, which a per-address limiter cannot stop and which needs a wider
  id, i.e. an `ets_web` change the constraint forbids; the alert sharing
  `NOTIFY_WEBHOOK_URL` with the guest-photo receiver, which may silently drop it;
  and the unwatched `ets_web` schema contract.
